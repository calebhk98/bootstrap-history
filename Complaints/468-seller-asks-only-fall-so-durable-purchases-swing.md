# Sellers may only cut their asks, so prices ratchet down and durable purchases swing

**Status:** partly - the store's sale reservation is now never below its own bid's price; the swing itself is the store treating in-use durable stock as its holding (found, fix tried, blocked by an existing bound); capacity planning at the post-addition price is not built

The seller-pricing rule built for Complaint 336 (`sim/economy/seller_pricing.py`, `seller_offers.py`, hooked into `year_goods.py`) lets a producer that moves the clearing price cut its ask against the market's book, but never raise it (a labelled heuristic standing in for many workshops that cannot hold goods back together). With cuts only, a durable good's price drifts down year after year. In the service-lives fixture, households keep buying well above wear long after their stock is filled, and the stock swings up and down from year to year instead of settling. Before the pricing hook, purchases fell to about the wear once the stock was full.

Evidence: `python3 -m sim.tests --only economy_service_lives` fails "it is not bought again in full": late purchases exceed the bound of a small multiple of late wear. Print the path with `python3 -c "from sim.tests import test_economy_service_lives as t; [print(year, [round(value, 1) for value in row[:3]]) for year, row in enumerate(t.years_of(t.durable_setup(), 13))]"`; running the same at the commit before the pricing merge (`git log --merges --grep profit-seeking-seller-pricing`) shows the settled path.

What it would take: the restraint half of the pricing build in `Complaints/reports/innovator-pricing-research.md`: a seller with market power may also raise its ask (or withhold) when that earns more, and capacity is judged at the price expected after the seller's own additions. The two-year cobweb swing the pricing agent reported in the coffee scenario is likely the same cause. Do not loosen the test's bound; it is catching the instability.

Related: 336.

## Findings while working it

- At the merged base the test passes: its bound is met because late purchases come out negative (households sell stock out), not because the stock settles. Print the path with the command above.
- In that fixture no producer moves the price (the external edge offers and bids as a fringe), so the seller choice never changes an ask, whether cuts-only or with raises. The swing is households' store of value (`households_store.py`; no complaint covers it yet): they offer held metal at the holding reservation, below the edge's bid, and bid for it again when the price is above.
- Remaining: derive the store's reservation against the same bid it later pays; plan capacity at the price expected after the producer's own additions (`sim/economy/producers.py`, owned by another change at the time).
- With raises allowed, the innovator scenario's price path after entry is a swing (the test now compares the settled level with the pre-entry level), and `economy_labour_core`'s no-training wage ratio is higher than with cuts only (measure by clamping the ceiling to the current ask in `seller_offers.py`); that test already failed without raises, so the raise does not cause it.

## Findings, second pass

- Reservation: `store_offers` now offers at no less than the price its own bid pays (`households_store.py`; unit test in `test_economy_service_lives`). On its own this does not change the path: the external edge bids at or above that price here, so the sale still clears.
- The churn is a double count. `store_holding` reports all held stock as the store, while the needs path (`_durable_ratio` in `households_orders.py`) also wants that stock in use. Whenever the store target (a share of wealth) is below the stock, it sells the "excess" that the needs path then buys back. Netting the in-use stock (flow times service life, band added) out of the store's holding removes every large sale and the negative purchases.
- That netting is not committed: it exposes that the stock is still filling in the thirteen-year window, so `test_it_is_not_bought_again_in_full` then fails by a few percent (late purchases about 2.6 times late wear against the bound of 2.5). The desired in-use stock is a year's flow times the service life, the flow follows the year's spending (income grows in the fixture), and the fill is limited by the year's spending, so the stock tracks a target that keeps rising. The old pass came from the sell-outs offsetting that. Measure: print the path with the command above, and the per-cohort desired stock by logging `in_use` in `goods_orders`.
- What would settle it: a durable's desired stock set from an expected (smoothed) flow rather than the year's spending, so a 20-year life does not multiply a one-year swing twenty times, and the store holding netted as above. The regression `test_late_holdings_settle_and_purchases_stay_near_wear_without_selling_out` pins it (expected failure until then).
- Capacity: `producers.plan` sizes runs at the expected price and the view carries no demand curve, so planning at the price after the producer's own additions needs the market's residual demand exposed to producers (the same book `seller_pricing` reads). Not built; the two expected failures in `test_economy_innovator_pricing` still fail honestly.
- Second pass, tried and not committed: smoothing the spending a durable's stock is sized on (a declared share of the gap closed a year) plus the netting above gives a smooth path with no sell-outs, and the settle regression passes, but `test_it_is_not_bought_again_in_full` still fails (late purchases about two and a half times its bound's margin: roughly 6400 against 5300 at the fastest smoothing tried, and no better at slower smoothing). Cause: the fixture's household income keeps growing, so the desired stock keeps rising and purchases are wear plus that growth; the bound holds only if the stock is full and flat by about year nine. Needs an owner decision on the bound or the fixture. Measure: print the path with the command above after applying the change.
