## 1. What the code does today

- `sim/world/merchant_terms.py` (standalone): `competition_markup_share(merchants)` is `MONOPOLY_MARKUP_SHARE / merchants`; `wait_years(round_trip_years, carriers)` is round trip over carriers; `redirect_share_per_year`; `capital_to_finance(own_capital, credit_room)` is own capital plus borrowing capped at `BORROWING_PER_OWN_CAPITAL` times own and at lenders' room.
- `sim/engine/foreign_traders.py` (engine layer, foreign trade by merchants): `_route_carriers` counts carriers on a route and treats each as one merchant's venture; `_trader_cycle_years` is voyage plus the wait; `_agent_cost_per_tonne` multiplies `AGENTS_PER_CARRIER` by the lift years per tonne, hours per person-year and the labour market's merchant wage; `merchant_class_capital` is merchants (from `labour.national_trade_population("merchant")`) times the modest-merchant kit (labourer-years times the labourer wage) plus `merchant_retained`, less the fleet's value; `merchant_capital_left` adds borrowing within `market_credit_room(MERCHANTS_BORROWER)`; `_retain_merchant_earnings` adds `SAVING_SHARE_OF_SURPLUS` (a households' heuristic in `sim/agents/saving.py`) times the markup earned.
- `MERCHANT_DENSITY` is a temporary heuristic in `sim/labour/labour_population.py` (merchants as a share of the urban population).
- The agent economy has its own merchants (`sim/economy/merchants.py`, `merchants_credit.py`, `merchants_shares.py`). They are arbitrageurs between market areas: bid where the expected price plus carriage, interest, spoilage and `MERCHANT_MARGIN_SHARE` is below the expected price elsewhere, sized by `MERCHANT_MARKET_SHARE`, `MERCHANT_GROUP_SHARE` and `MERCHANT_SOURCE_SHARE` of expected volumes, and borrowing under `MERCHANT_LEVERAGE_LIMIT`. They have no ships, agents or sailing calendar. The foreign-trade merchants of the engine and these are two implementations of one actor; section 7 covers that.
- Counterparty work: Complaint 382 (every posting names a counterparty; partner economies at its step 4) and 407 (foreign countries have no labour market or output of their own). A foreign market's price, wage and demand are today the home ones rescaled, which bounds every derivation below that reads the destination.

## 2. MONOPOLY_MARKUP_SHARE: derive from the destination's residual demand, then delete the constant

**Derivation.** A merchant chooses the quantity (or reservation price) that maximises `(price_at_destination(quantity) - cost_per_unit) * quantity`, where `cost_per_unit` is purchase price plus freight, interest over the cycle, loss and agents. `price_at_destination(quantity)` is the destination market's inverse residual demand: buyers' schedule minus supply already on offer from rivals and local makers, the dominant-firm-with-fringe rule of `innovator-pricing-research.md` section 2.2. The markup is an outcome, not an input. For a lone merchant facing locally constant elasticity it reduces to the textbook inverse-elasticity rule that the constant stands in for; with several merchants choosing quantities it falls toward cost (Cournot), which is what `competition_markup_share` imitates by 1 over the number. Both limits come out of the same maximiser, so neither the constant nor the 1-over-n form is needed.

**State it reads.** The destination market's book (buyers' schedule and offered reservations, the `market_curves.summarize` rows), the merchants' own cost build-up, and rival merchants' committed cargo on the route (the `RouteShares` idea in `merchants_shares.py`, which already tracks what rivals have bid into a destination). For the engine layer, the same book must exist for the foreign market, which is Complaint 382 step 4 and 407: until a partner economy has its own demand schedule, the elasticity can only be read from the home market rescaled, and a label must say so.

**Why it is not just the pricing build.** The pricing research asks for a seller who knows the book. A merchant is a seller at the destination and a buyer at the origin, so the merchant's maximiser needs both books: residual demand at the destination and the origin's supply schedule (what its own purchases do to the price it pays, monopsony). The pricing stage 1 function (residual demand from a book) serves the first; the second is the mirror, a residual supply function. Ask the pricing build to expose it symmetrically.

**Sourced figures for checks (relationships, not targets).**
- The commenda's fixed profit split is a contractual share, not a market markup: the unilateral form gave the investor three quarters of profit and the travelling partner one quarter, the bilateral form two thirds of capital from the investor and one third from the traveller with profit split evenly [read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commenda, summary of the article; the Genoese 1156 example is [snippet] from a search result]. It shows how a venture's profit divides between capital and labour; it does not give the market markup.
- Venetian merchant profit on invested capital in the first half of the fifteenth century was estimated at about 9.6 to 10 percent from the Soranzo fraterna account book (1406 to 1434), judged "a rather moderate estimate", against forced-loan yields as the interest benchmark [read, abstract only: https://journals.eco-vector.com/0131-8780/article/view/628656]. Use as a relationship: long-run merchant profit over capital sits a small multiple above the interest rate, not many times it.
- English East India Company separate-stock voyages 1601 to 1612: profits reported "seldom below 100 percent" per voyage, 171 percent over the first voyages in one account and 95 percent on the first two, falling to 87.5 percent on four joint-stock voyages [snippet: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/East_India_Company, via a search summary; the 1911 text was not opened]. Voyages lasted years, so the annual rate is far lower; this is the rent of a chartered monopoly with plunder in the first voyage, a case for the monopoly end of the maximiser (a lone seller with a legal bar to rivals).
- Venetian state galleys: contractors received a monopoly for two months after departure on the goods agreed for the route, with freight a tax of a few percent of the goods transported [read, summary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muda_(convoy)]. Again a monopoly case, with the charter's price set by auction among nobles, so the auction bid is the rent captured by the state.

**Relationship tests.** (1) With one merchant and a steep residual demand the markup share equals the inverse elasticity at the optimum within solver tolerance. (2) The markup falls monotonically as equal merchants are added and approaches zero margin over cost as their number grows. (3) A flatter destination demand (more substitutes) lowers the markup at fixed merchants. (4) Raising the origin's supply elasticity raises the price merchants pay less than a steeper supply would (monopsony). (5) Merchant profit over capital tends toward the interest rate where entry is free (relationship to the Venetian moderate-profit case), above it where a charter or a closed sea limits entry.

## 3. AGENTS_PER_CARRIER: a house's staffing from what it must do

**Derivation.** A house needs a person at each end for as long as cargo is bought, loaded, received and sold; the hours are the cargo's handling and selling time, not a headcount per ship. Staffing then follows from (a) tonnes handled per year at each end, (b) the time a tonne takes to buy and load, and to sell into demand (section 5), and (c) the span of control a principal can supervise by letter. Agents' cost per tonne is hours per tonne times the labour market's merchant wage, which is what the code already does for the wage; the change is replacing the "two per carrier" count by hours per tonne that fall out of the selling-time model. A house with several carriers spreads fixed presence (a factor resident at each port, who must be there however many ships call) over more tonnes, so cost per tonne falls with the house's volume at a port: a fixed-plus-variable form where the fixed part is one resident agent per port served, the variable part is handling hours per tonne.

**State it reads.** Tonnes per year per port from the freight lift ledger (`foreign_lift_capacity_tonnes`, and the domestic flow ledger of `freight-remains-research.md` section 4); the merchant wage quote; the number of ports a house serves (route legs); the selling time of section 5.

**Sourced figures.**
- Roman shipowners (navicularii) were often also traders (negotiatores); a shipowner not sailing appointed a representative, the magister navis [read: https://www.romanports.org/en/articles/human-interest/657-the-roman-shipowners.html]. So a Roman house's agents were largely the ship's own master, in line with a venture carried by one principal plus a delegate rather than resident factors at both ends.
- Geniza merchants organised through partnerships, usually between co-religionists and often reinforced by kin, investing capital and engaging agents from one end of the known world to the other [snippet: https://genizalab.princeton.edu/indian-ocean-documents-cairo-geniza and a Goitein-based search summary]. The agent was typically a partner or relative resident at the far port, which is the "fixed presence per port" term.
- The Datini company expanded from Florence and Prato to branch offices (fondaci) in Pisa, Genoa, Avignon, Barcelona, Valencia and Palma, organised as a group of partnerships in the manner of a holding company, in 1383 [snippet: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Datini]. Staff counts per branch were not found. The Medici: each branch separately capitalised, the Florentine centre holding the majority and the local manager a minority stake, the manager paid a larger profit share than his equity if the branch did well [snippet: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medici_Bank, via search summary]. A figure of about forty employees for the Medici bank was returned by the search and not checked; do not use it. Pattern: one manager per port with a profit share, the incentive solution to supervising by letter.
- Champagne fairs: merchants came to the fair, so agents were few and travelling, with a fixed settlement period at the end of each fair [read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champagne_fairs].

**Relationship tests.** (1) Agent cost per tonne falls as a house's tonnes at a port rise (fixed presence spread). (2) It rises with the merchant wage and with the selling time. (3) A route of one port at each end costs at least two residents' time per year regardless of how few carriers sail. (4) With the labour market's merchant wage doubled, cost per tonne doubles at fixed hours.

## 4. One merchant per carrier, and ships owned, chartered and shared

**Derivation.** Make ownership of a carrier a set of shares and the merchant a separate actor that charters freight. Three roles are separate in the sources: the owner (receives freight or hire), the operator (master, runs the voyage), and the shipper (owns the cargo, pays freight). A house is a merchant holding cargo in several carriers and shares in several carriers. Spreading is a choice: a merchant maximising expected return less a risk penalty splits capital across ships so one loss does not ruin it, limited by the fixed cost of each additional consignment and by the minimum shipment that an agent can handle. Risk pricing is the capital-markets insurance stage (below).

**State it reads.** Carrier ownership records (shares by actor), the hull loss rate (`HULL_LOSS_PER_THOUSAND_KM` over the sea legs, already used in `_route_cargo_loss_share`), freight rates per tonne, the capital market's premium for risk.

**Sourced figures.**
- Hanse merchants in the fifteenth century often owned more than one ship or held a share in several ships through partnerships; large vessels were financed by co-ownership [snippet: search summary naming the Hansa and the partenreederei]. Partenreederei: part-owners share proceeds and debts proportionally, with unlimited liability, dating to the Middle Ages and related to the Dutch partenrederij [read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partenreederei].
- Genoa: a 1248 document records the purchase of a sixth part of a ship; shares reduced the risk of loss, as in Marseilles [snippet: https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/Halsall/source/1248pship3.asp, via search summary]. The term "carati" for shares, and the Venetian measure of galley cargo space in carats rented to individual merchants, appear in the Wikipedia muda article [read: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muda_(convoy)], where contractors won the galleys by auction and sublet cargo space by the carat.
- Song and Yuan China: investors usually divided their investment among many ships, and each ship had many investors; classes of participant included shipowners, shippers, charterers, financiers and small merchants [snippet: search summary of Clark and Schottenhammer; not opened].
- Rome: the owner was often also the trader; contract rules gave tax relief to owners of one very large vessel or of five or more mid-size vessels, showing both single-ship owners and small fleets existed [read: romanports shipowners page above]. Roman-law ship-owner associations (societates) financed their own winter expeditions [read: https://romanports.org/en/articles/human-interest/156-winter-shipping.html].

**Relationship tests.** (1) With a positive hull loss rate and a risk penalty, the optimal number of ships a merchant spreads over rises with its capital and falls with the fixed cost per consignment. (2) The ruin probability of a merchant falls as it spreads at equal expected return. (3) Freight paid equals the carrier owners' revenue, conserved to the unit. (4) A carrier with many part-owners and one operator pays each part-owner its share of net hire; no engine code names a house.

## 5. The wait: time to sell into demand, and a sailing season

**Derivation.** Cargo money is tied up from purchase to sale, and the cycle has four parts: waiting for a sailing at the origin, the voyage, waiting for a market at the destination, and selling the cargo into demand. Today only the first and second exist. Two additions:
1. **Sailing season from geography.** A route's usable months follow from the winds and seas on its legs: a closed sea or a monsoon window. Sailings per year then equal the carriers' round trips that fit in the open window; a cargo bought outside the window waits for the next one. A window comes from the leg's climate data (prevailing wind reversal for monsoon legs, winter gale frequency for the Mediterranean), not authored dates (CLAUDE.md 4.1). `sim/geography/` owns the climate; the route's legs would carry an open-fraction-of-year and an opening phase.
2. **Selling time from demand.** Selling a cargo of size `cargo_tonnes` into a market whose usual yearly volume is `market_tonnes_per_year` takes about `cargo_tonnes / (share_of_market * market_tonnes_per_year)` of a year if the merchant will not depress the price. With the residual-demand rule of section 2 the merchant instead chooses a selling rate: faster sale lowers the price, slower sale costs interest and spoilage. The optimal sale period is an output of the same maximiser with an interest term, and a perishable good shortens it. This unifies the markup and the wait: slow selling is how a lone merchant holds price.

**State it reads.** Leg climate and season data in `sim/geography/`; the destination market's expected yearly volume (`expected_volumes` in the agent economy; `market_memory`); the interest rate (`market_rate`); the good's spoilage rate (`cargo_cost.spoilage_rates`).

**Sourced figures.**
- Mediterranean in Roman times: Vegetius gives a safe season, an uncertain shoulder and a closed sea from about mid-November to early March [read: romanports winter shipping page, which gives closed seas 13 November to 6 March]; another account gives normal shipping 26 May to 14 September, outside limits 10 March to 10 November [snippet: search summary, source not identified]. The two summaries disagree on the dates, so use only the relationship: a closed window of several months, with off-season sailing as a risk taken by private merchants when scarcity raised prices. The Wikipedia article on mare clausum gives November to March for the Roman period [read, summary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_clausum]. Large grain ships between Ostia and Alexandria took about 18 to 25 days with favourable winds and 50 to 60 days against [read: romanports page].
- Indian Ocean: Geniza ships sailed from Aden to India in late summer or autumn soon after the ships from Egypt arrived, and returned between winter and April [snippet: search summary; Margariti's page failed to load]. Relationship: one round trip a year, fixed by the monsoon, so the sailings per carrier per year are at most one however fast the ship.
- Venetian mude: ships usually left Venice in spring and returned in autumn, one muda a year per route; the Aigues-Mortes muda stopped for eighteen days at its destination, and the contractors' monopoly on listed goods lasted two months after departure [read, summary: Muda page]. Relationship: a destination stay of weeks, so selling time is weeks to a season for a convoy cargo, not years.
- Champagne fairs: six fairs a year in a circuit, each opening with a settling-in week and then days for cloth, leather and weighed goods, with a four-day final settlement of accounts [read: Champagne fairs page; its two statements on a fair's length conflict, so the length is not used]. Relationship: a fixed calendar of selling windows, with settlement only at set dates; a merchant that misses the fair waits for the next.

**Relationship tests.** (1) With the sailing window halved at fixed carriers, the expected wait doubles (the wait is the closed fraction plus the round trip gap). (2) Selling time rises with cargo over market volume and falls with the number of competing sellers. (3) A good with a higher spoilage rate shows a shorter optimal sale period and a smaller cargo. (4) A route whose legs have no closed season has no season term (the code today). (5) Money tied up is voyage plus waits plus selling time; capital needed for a flow rises with each, in proportion.

## 6. The class's capital and lenders' room

**Derivation.** Replace `merchants * modest-merchant kit * MERCHANT_DENSITY` by the merchants' own balance sheets: each merchant actor has cash and stock; the class capital is their sum. Merchant numbers come from the labour market's trade allocation (people choosing the trade because its return beats the alternative), not a density. The initial capital is an initial condition per civilisation (allowed by CLAUDE.md 4.1) taken from data the start file already carries, such as the class's share of wealth, while later capital is accumulated or lost by the merchants' own results. Borrowing goes through the capital market: a merchant's loan request is a claim in the same pool as every other borrower's, so lenders' room is read after merchants' loans are added. In the engine today `market_credit_room` subtracts "others" from capacity but merchant borrowing in the foreign trade code is not booked as a loan, so the room is overstated; the fix is booking each flow's financing as a claim (the capital-markets stage 0 claim kind) so the pool sees it.

**State it reads.** Merchant actors' purses and stocks (the agent economy already has `Merchant.capital_base`, and `merchants_credit.credit_request` sized under `MERCHANT_LEVERAGE_LIMIT`), `record.loans`, `record.capacity` and the credit market's answer (`economy.agent_credit_room`), and the capital market's institutions (bank, equity, insurance, in `capital-markets-build-research.md` section 5).

**Sourced figures.**
- Genoese commenda and Venetian colleganza: the unilateral commenda put all capital with the investor; the bilateral form two thirds from the investor and one third from the traveller [read: Commenda page]. First documented Venetian colleganza 1073, evidence of use from the tenth century [read: same]. So merchants' own capital was a minority of what they traded with, the rest coming from investors who shared profit and loss; `BORROWING_PER_OWN_CAPITAL` of two matches the bilateral split of capital, but the investor's return was a profit share, not interest.
- Roman sea loan: the Muziris papyrus preserves a loan agreement to finance a trade venture to South India and the assessed value of a cargo imported on the Hermapollon; the Arabarchs (contractors collecting duties on Indian Ocean goods) provided financial support to merchants in this trade [snippet: https://rmblf.be/2020/06/06/publication-federico-de-romanis-the-indo-roman-pepper-trade-and-the-muziris-papyrus/ and a De Romanis search summary]. A search summary gave the cargo value as almost 10,000,000 denarii, which does not match the recalled sesterce figure in the literature and is [disputed]; the two PDF fetches failed, so no loan rate, term or cargo value is used here. The relationship worth testing is that the loan was repayable only if the voyage returned (a bottomry shape), which is the capital-markets insurance stage 6.
- Medici: each branch separately capitalised with the centre holding the majority and a local manager a minority [snippet: Medici Bank page]. Pattern: capital pooled by a head house and lent to a branch, not drawn from the manager alone.
- EIC separate stock: the subscribers individually bore a voyage's cost and took its whole profit, with each fleet separately financed and kept separate accounts; from 1612 joint stock [snippet: 1911 Britannica via search summary]. Capital was raised per venture before it was raised for a standing fleet.

**Relationship tests.** (1) Total loans in the pool equal the sum of borrowers' debts including merchants'; lenders' room falls as merchants borrow. (2) Merchants' capital after a lost cargo falls by the loss less insurance; a class that lost many cargoes borrows less next year. (3) Capital rises with profitable trade and the number of merchants responds through the labour market with a lag. (4) No capital figure depends on a density constant.

## 7. Retained earnings at the households' saving share

**Derivation.** A merchant's earnings are its owner's income; what is kept is the owner's saving decision, made by the same saving rule as any household's, from the owner's own income, wealth and expected returns, not a constant (and in particular not the households' `SAVING_SHARE_OF_SURPLUS` applied to the markup). A merchant in the agent economy already does a version of this: `close_year` pays the owner a share of profit (`MERCHANT_PROFIT_PAYOUT_SHARE`, a heuristic) and keeps the rest as working capital. The derivation is to use the owner's household record: the payout is the owner's income, and the owner's consumption and saving come from the household layer; reinvestment is the owner lending or staking money, which happens only if the return on trade beats the owner's other uses (loans, land). Reinvestment then responds to the interest rate.

**State it reads.** The owner's household income and wealth, the capital market's rate, the route's expected return.

**Sourced figures.** Documented savings behaviour of merchant houses is thin in what was found. The Medici managers' larger profit share than their equity gave them [snippet: Medici Bank page] and the Datini partnership form [snippet] show profit shares fixed by contract and capital left in the partnership for its term, which is a retention rule set by the partnership deed, not the owner's budget. Treat retention as a contract term (the partnership's duration and the withdrawal rules) as well as a saving rule.

**Relationship tests.** (1) The share of profit retained rises with the expected return on trade relative to the owner's alternative, and with the partnership's lock-in. (2) A merchant whose owner is poorer pays out more. (3) One owner's household income conserves: payout plus retained equals profit.

## 8. Staged build plan, with dependencies

Order is chosen so each stage can be tested alone. Every stage opens with a failing regression test (CLAUDE.md 6) and `python3 -m sim.tests.fingerprint` before and after, because the foreign-trade terms feed the simulation.

**Stage 0: measure.** Add tests that record, for a sample route, today's markup, wait, agent cost per tonne and class capital, as a baseline for the relationships above; label each derived term's remaining heuristic in `python3 sim/constants.py --burndown`. No behaviour change. Also decide where the engine and agent-economy merchants converge (question 1 of section 10); this controls where later stages are written.

**Stage 1: markup from residual demand.** Depends on the pricing build exposing residual demand from a market book (pricing stage 1) and, for the monopsony side, residual supply. Replace `competition_markup_share` by the merchant's maximiser over the destination book and rivals' committed cargo; remove `MONOPOLY_MARKUP_SHARE`. For a foreign destination this needs the partner's book: until Complaint 382 step 4 (partner economies) and 407 provide it, read the home book rescaled and label the substitute `temporary_heuristic`. Tests: section 2 relationships 1 to 5.

**Stage 2: selling time and sailing season.** Depends on stage 1 (the sale-rate choice is part of the same maximiser) and on `sim/geography/` exposing leg climate. Add the closed fraction and phase per leg and the selling-time term in `_trader_cycle_years`. Tests: section 5 relationships. Another agent owns `sim/geography/` data at the time of the freight research (`freight-remains-research.md` section 5); check ownership before touching it.

**Stage 3: staffing from handling hours.** Depends on stage 2 for selling hours and on the freight flow ledger for tonnes per port. Replace `AGENTS_PER_CARRIER` by resident presence per port plus handling hours per tonne. Tests: section 3 relationships.

**Stage 4: ownership shares and merchants as separate actors from carriers.** Depends on the carrier market of `freight-remains-research.md` section 4.2 (freight paid to the carrier's owner) and on Complaint 382's counterparty rule (freight and shares need a real payee, not `edge:legacy`). Tests: section 4 relationships.

**Stage 5: capital from balance sheets and the pool.** Depends on capital-markets stage 0 (claim kind, proportional clearing) so merchant borrowing is a claim; on stage 6 insurance for the risk term of stage 4 (until it lands, spreading uses the hull loss rate only). Tests: section 6 relationships.

**Stage 6: retention from the owner's saving.** Depends on the households' saving rule maturing past `SAVING_SHARE_OF_SURPLUS`. Tests: section 7 relationships.

What can be started now: stages 0 and 3 partly (the labour side), and stage 5's pool booking. What waits: stage 1 for the pricing build, stages 1, 5 for the counterparty work.

## 10. Open questions

1. Two merchant implementations exist (engine foreign trade and the agent economy's `merchants.py`). Is the target one actor type, with foreign trade as the agent economy's merchants reaching foreign market areas? That removes duplicate heuristics (the two hold different margin, share and borrowing constants) but depends on 382's partner economies. Owner decision.
2. The markup maximiser makes a lone merchant restrain sales. Should a staple route allow this (as Question 2 of the pricing report asks of producers), or should a staple's destination demand floor stop it?
3. Where does the season live: on route legs from climate (geography package), or on carriers as a capability (a ship type that can sail in a closed sea)? Both are plausible; the second makes ships a technology matter (the winter exception in Rome was risk, not impossibility).
4. How does a merchant's time-to-sell relate to the fair or convoy calendar when sale dates are fixed by an institution? A fair is a market-clearing date; the yearly step has no within-year clock. Does the foreign market clear more than once a year?
5. Charters and monopolies (Venetian galley auctions, EIC): is a legal monopoly a rule an actor (a state) can create, with merchants bidding for it? The auction price would then capture the markup rent, which is a relationship worth testing but needs a state mechanism.
6. The commenda's profit split is contract-defined: should merchants' profit sharing with investors be a term of a loan-like claim (equity stage 5 of the capital-markets plan) or a share of venture profit with no fixed interest?
7. Calibration: all figures here are checks on relationships, not targets. Which documented voyage accounts will be used to check the order of magnitude of the markup, and who verifies them as read?
