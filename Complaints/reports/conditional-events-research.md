# Conditional historical events: what causes them, and how data can state it

Research for Complaint 266, item 2 (events evaluate their own preconditions in data). No code or data was changed. Branch: `conditional-events-research`, cut from `structural-dedupe-and-owner-decisions`. A later pass (branch `verify-conditional-events-sources`) opened sources for the attributions below.

Source tags used below: **read** means the page or document was opened in full or in the part cited and the citation given supports the attribution; **snippet** means the claim was in a web search result that was not opened; **recalled** means it is from memory and has not been re-checked; **corrected** in a row means a source that was opened says something different from the original text, and the row now states what it says; **disputed** names two sides that the sources show disagreeing. Most `read` entries are Wikipedia pages: they are secondary summaries that name few historians, so a `read` on a Wikipedia page confirms what the encyclopaedia says the causes are, not what a named scholar argued. Three scholarly sources were opened: Esmonde Cleary's review of Harper, Schubert's article (which summarises Rives), and Teeter's review of Heather. Scholarly books named in the table (Goldstone, Turchin and Nefedov, Halsall, Hassig, Duncan-Jones, Butcher and Ponting) were not opened except where a row says so. Treat every `snippet` and `recalled` claim as needing a read pass before it is cited in game text.

## 0. What exists today

- A dated event is a `hazards` entry in `data/civilizations/<civ>.json` with a `years` window and effect fields (`staff_loss`, `sack_chance`, `output_factor`, `real_erosion`, `values`). `Sim._shocks` (`sim/engine/society_hazards.py`) applies every hazard whose window is open, in file order, with a fixed random-draw order that the fingerprint depends on.
- Two different things share the word "chance". The event's occurrence is not random (the window opens on its year). `sack_chance` and `staff_loss` are yearly rolls inside the window, softened by `hazard_relief` and the `hazard_counters` declared on tech nodes (`sim/engine/mechanics.py`; `requires_running` on a counter, `_counter_requirements`). The design below separates onset (does the event begin) from effect (what it does once under way).
- A few hazards carry `condition`: `{field, requires_all: [tech ids], outcome: avert|alter, alter_scale, met_message, unmet_message}`, resolved once per hazard by `_resolve_hazard_condition`. Present in Rome (the crossings and the sack of Rome, the Arab conquests), England (dearth of the 1590s, great frost of 1740, Great Fire), Mexica (wars of independence). It tests what the player built, which is a counter, not what the world's state is, which is a cause. It cannot express "the fiscal crisis did not happen because the treasury was sound".
- The divergence screen (`sim/ui/proto/screen_divergence.py`) lists dated events with status and a hard-coded `causes_checked: False`, and says in `cannot_know` that no event states its causes.
- Complaint 180 (closed): dated crises stay until the dynamic systems can produce them, and are not replaced by random draws. This report keeps that: nothing here removes a date, it adds stated causes and reports which are unmodelled.
- Complaint 250 (not found in `Complaints/`; the number appears in 266 and 180 text only): the Mexica invasion rolls at a very high yearly sack chance for three years in every run.

## 2. Data design for preconditions

Principles: the engine never names a content id (4.7); a cause is a named reading of simulator state, given in data; the engine knows a registry of readings, not events. Nothing here adds a price table or a result tuned to history (4.1, 4.5).

### 2.1 Event shape

An event keeps its `years` and effects. It gains three optional blocks. Example for the third-century entry (illustrative; names of readings are proposals):

```json
{
  "name": "Third century crisis",
  "years": [235, 284],
  "output_factor": 0.62,
  "sack_chance": 0.16,
  "onset": {
    "mode": "scheduled",
    "base_rate_per_year": 0.0,
    "causes": [
      {"id": "army_unpaid", "label": "the army is not being paid",
       "when": {"read": "state.unfunded_share", "of": "army", "gt": 0.15},
       "weight": 1.2, "modelled": true,
       "source": {"ref": "Wikipedia, Crisis of the Third Century", "tag": "snippet"}},
      {"id": "debased_coin", "when": {"read": "state.coin_metal_kept", "lt": 0.9},
       "weight": 0.8, "modelled": true},
      {"id": "frontier_pressure", "modelled": false,
       "weight": 1.0, "note": "needs a neighbour polity with an army that presses a frontier"},
      {"id": "epidemic_load", "when": {"read": "population.disease_burden", "gt": 1.2},
       "weight": 0.6, "modelled": true},
      {"id": "legitimacy", "modelled": false, "weight": 0.8}
    ]
  },
  "forms": [
    {"id": "fragmentation", "when": {"all": [{"cause": "army_unpaid"}, {"cause": "frontier_pressure"}]},
     "output_factor": 0.55, "sack_chance": 0.2},
    {"id": "contained", "when": {"not": {"cause": "army_unpaid"}},
     "output_factor": 0.92}
  ],
  "fails_when": {"cause_score_below": 0.25}
}
```

An event without `onset` behaves exactly as today. This keeps the owner's decision in 180: `mode: "scheduled"` fires on the window as now.

### 2.2 Expression vocabulary

A small tree, evaluated by one function with no knowledge of content:

- Leaves: `{"read": "<reading name>", "of": "<role or key>"}` returns a number. `{"const": number}`. `{"has": [node ids]}` (generalises the existing `requires_all`; node ids are data, so allowed). `{"cause": "<cause id in this event>"}` returns whether that cause currently holds.
- Comparisons: `gt`, `ge`, `lt`, `le`, `eq` against a constant or another reading.
- Combinators: `all`, `any`, `not`.
- Trend: `{"read": ..., "change_over_years": n}` for a reading's change since n years ago. Needs a short history ring per reading; only readings that declare `trend: true` keep one.
- Degree: each cause evaluates to a held degree between zero and one, from a linear ramp between `start` and `full` thresholds, so a cause rises gradually instead of flipping. A bare comparison is a step (degree zero or one).

Readings live in one registry, filled by each walled package through its `api.py` (`docs/architecture/PACKAGE_WALLS.md`): the engine asks the registry for the name. A new cause is one registry entry plus data. A reading's return type and unit are declared with it, and `simulator.py validate` rejects an event naming an unknown reading, a role it cannot resolve, or a comparison of mismatched units. The `of` roles ("army", "home government", "neighbour with border") are resolved by the scenario, not by the engine; a civilisation file names which foreign actor plays the role.

### 2.3 Hazard rather than date

For an event in `hazard` mode (not before the owner decides, see section 4), the yearly onset probability is:

    onset_probability_per_year = 1 - exp(-(base_rate_per_year * exp(sum_over_causes(weight_of_cause * held_degree_of_cause))))

Each cause has a signed weight: positive raises, negative lowers (a sound treasury is a cause with negative weight, which is how a cause "lowers" the event). `base_rate_per_year` is the background rate when no named cause holds. The window `years` is kept and gets a role of its own: in `scheduled` mode it fires the event; in `hazard` mode it is `prior_window`, used only to report drift and to validate the ensemble (4.2), never to fire. One random draw per hazard-mode event per year, taken in file order after the existing draws so the fingerprint for scheduled events does not move. Causes marked `modelled: false` contribute nothing to the sum, and are listed in the report as missing. That gives the interim rule: a scheduled event reports which causes are missing; a hazard-mode event with missing causes is a validation failure, so no event goes hazard-mode while a named major cause is missing.

### 2.4 Changing form or failing

- `forms`: ordered list; the first form whose `when` holds is applied at onset, and the plain event fields are the default. A form overrides effect fields only, so the effect code in `society_hazards.py` is unchanged. The chosen form is recorded and told once, like `met_message` is today.
- `fails_when` (or `averts_when`): evaluated at onset. If true, the event does not occur, and the player is told once with the cause that was absent. The present `condition` block is expressible as `averts_when: {"has": [...]}` and migrates by rewriting, with its messages kept.
- Duration: a form may shorten the window (`ends_when`), so an event can end early when its causes stop holding.

### 2.5 The divergence screen reads the same conditions

For each dated event the screen evaluates every cause and returns, per cause: `id`, `label`, `status` (`holds`, `does not hold`, `not modelled`), the reading's value now against its threshold, and the source tag. `causes_checked` becomes true only if no cause is `not modelled`. Add `causes_missing` (the labels), `forms_now` (which form would apply today) and, for a scheduled event whose causes mostly do not hold, a plain statement that the date fires regardless. The screen shows fog the same way it does today (name hidden for upcoming events under fog). Because the screen and the engine share one evaluator, a tester can see why an event fired. Late-game empires with a sound treasury and no frontier would show "fires although its causes do not hold", which is the exact readout Complaint 266 asked for.

## 3. Migration path

Each stage ships with its own test and keeps behaviour for unmigrated events.

0. **Schema and reporting only.** Add the optional `onset.causes` block, validate it in `simulator.py validate`, and make the divergence screen list `causes_missing` and each cause's `modelled` flag. No readings are evaluated. Test: the validator rejects a malformed cause; the screen shows every migrated event with missing causes; `python3 -m sim.tests.fingerprint check` shows no change.
1. **Registry and evaluation, report only.** Build the reading registry with the readings that exist (state unfunded share, army against wanted, coin metal kept, debt against ceiling, disease burden, nutrition ratio, literacy, wage and price index). The screen evaluates causes. Behaviour unchanged. Test: a run with a funded army reports the army-pay cause as not holding; fingerprint unchanged.
2. **Gate by cause for events that already have a `condition`.** Rewrite the existing `condition` blocks into `averts_when` and `forms`, with the same messages. Test: `sim/tests/test_historical_events.py` passes unchanged in outcome; add a test that a cause-based gate averts a hazard. Stays within 180: these events already could change.
3. **First hazard-mode event.** Convert `Currency debasement` (Rome), because the economy already decides debasement from unfunded need (`government_coinage.py`), so its date is a duplicate of a mechanism that exists. Retire the dated `real_erosion` for a civilisation whose coin regime is struck coin, and watch for double counting. Test: across a batch of seeds, debasement appears in some runs and not others, and appears earlier in runs with a larger army and thinner treasury. This is the first place where 180's "debasement and inflation from the economy work" is delivered.
4. **Plagues once an epidemic mechanism exists.** After pathogen introduction through contact is modelled, convert plague events: onset hazard rises with route connectivity and density. Test: no plague without contact; the Mexica contact epidemic needs an Old World contact event first.
5. **Foreign neighbour polities, then invasions and sacks.** After multiplayer and neighbour actors exist (180 says sacks come after multiplayer), the Gothic, Lombard, Spanish and Hunnic events take frontier pressure, allied city-states and technology gap as readings. Test: an invader without allies and technology advantage loses more often.
6. **Ensemble validation.** Run many seeds with no intervention and compare distributions (4.2): onset year spread, share of runs with a crisis, population and urbanisation ranges. No test asserts a dated event. A baseline that reproduces a dated plague in every run is evidence of cheating and should fail the check.

First increment: stages 0 and 1 together are small and change no behaviour. Stage 3 is the first behaviour change and the only one with an existing mechanism behind it.

## 4. Open questions for the owner

1. Is "this event fires although none of its modelled causes hold" acceptable under 180 for as long as the dated mode lasts, provided the screen says so? Or should an event with all modelled causes absent already be skippable (a softer step than full hazard mode)?
2. Should unmodelled causes be allowed in a hazard-mode event at all? The proposal says no, which keeps hazard mode back until the neighbour and epidemic mechanisms exist. The alternative is to allow a labelled temporary base rate for the missing cause (a 4.4 heuristic tag).
3. Where do events that are not caused by anything the model could have (earthquakes, the Great Fire) belong: stay dated, become yearly random events, or be dropped?
4. How should the Mexica invasion read for players? The research suggests three causes with different tractability (allies, epidemic, technology). Is "you cannot stop the conquest, only protect what you know" still the design intent once the allied-city-state cause is a lever a player can pull (for instance by not appearing as a tribute collector)?
5. Do the value shifts (`w_religious_rigidity` and the others) after an event stay attached to the event, or should they move to be the effect of a cause (for instance a loyalty or supplication rite following legitimacy stress)? The Decian edict is currently only a note and a `values` field.
6. Elite overproduction needs a definition of "elite" and "positions". Is the stratum model the right basis, with offices from `officials_kept`, or does the owner want a separate measure?
7. Is a per-event source tag (snippet, read, recalled) wanted in the data, and should game text ever cite the sources? If yes, a read pass of each is required first.
8. Who decides the cause weights? They are 4.4 heuristics until fitted; the ensemble in stage 6 could fit them, but then they encode a tuned relationship rather than a derived one. Is fitting weights to distributions acceptable under 4.1?
