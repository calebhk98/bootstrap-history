## Year 100 AD — arrival and setup, zero elapsed years

### Observed state
Later Han; poor scholar; fog on; immortal founder; transistor target; 500-year horizon ending 600 AD. Cash 750,651, recurring net −2,160/year, 2,000 available founder hours. No employees or running operations. 109 technologies granted, none built by me; 11 target-route nodes already present.

### What I think
The premise is strong: knowledge alone does not supply tools, skilled labor, materials, or operating income. Making completed works distinct from running ventures is a promising economic choice. Local labor reach, training, political exposure, material throughput, and historical hazards all appear in help. Their actual behavior remains untested.

Setup is understandable but not frictionless. Civilisation descriptions and irreversible-choice warnings are good. Launching through Python, remembering commands, interpreting currency and workforce abbreviations, and managing save paths require effort a polished player interface could remove. Help is comprehensive but too sprawling for a first encounter. An incorrect options alias is a concrete documentation defect.

The starter-finance narrative needs checking: "a few months' subsistence" does not obviously match 750,651 cash against a displayed −2,160 annual recurring net. I have not opened the full ledger; this is a discrepancy to investigate, not a proven economy exploit.

### Difficulty forecast
Basic command entry looks easy. Choosing economically sound projects under fog looks much harder. No construction, purchases, hiring, or time advancement have been tested. I expect mistakes and a slow opening. I tentatively expect useful early achievements and an income operation before reaching the ultimate target. I do not yet have grounds to predict a win.

The target menu gives transistors a 142-year ideal floor; the horizon menu calls 400 years tight and 500 standard. That makes 150 years an aggressive benchmark. It does not prove such a finish impossible, particularly with civilisation-granted prerequisites; the scope and civilisation-specific meaning of the floor are not yet established.

### Real-world comparison — opinion, not game evidence
An actual lone newcomer would face much more than a technology dependency graph: language, shelter, trust, patrons, access to workshops, practical tacit skill, measuring instruments, repeatability, quality control, and sustained cooperation. Remembering a device's principle would not mean being able to manufacture it. My forecast is that gaining credibility and building a durable organization would be a larger opening challenge than choosing inventions.

The game advertises several relevant constraints and could capture the shape of the problem well. I am skeptical that numerical reputation and aggregate trade-hours alone could represent negotiating legitimacy or manufacturing reliability. I have not seen enough gameplay to determine how deeply it handles those. Immortality changes the thought experiment substantially: it removes succession and personal lifespan as hard constraints. Fog challenges the player's planning but is not a literal equivalent of a knowledgeable inventor forgetting what modern technologies depend on.

### Mods and features I currently want
1. Better player interface and a fog-compatible discovery notebook: known dependencies, observations, experiments, unresolved questions, and personal goals without hidden-tree spoilers.
2. Civilisation-specific kits and money descriptions using concrete examples of food, rent, wages, and tools.
3. Optional practical-prototype and quality-control challenges: yields, failed replication, calibration, maintenance, and reproducibility. Check existing gameplay before deciding these are absent.
4. Optional social integration and succession: language learning, local collaborators, apprentice knowledge transfer, and institutions that can survive the founder. Check what training, reputation, corpus, and mortality already provide first.

I would improve usability before adding more difficulty. Additional commands are not automatically additional depth.

### Next plan
Read `available` and `money`; inspect relevant `why` descriptions. Learn the economy's actual units and recurring costs. Choose a modest, affordable income or foundational project. Advance carefully, record results, and change strategy based on observed bottlenecks. No rush/start-all shortcuts planned.

### Current feeling
Interested and cautiously optimistic about learning the system; skeptical of claiming mastery from setup alone. The interface is manageable, but some of the work is remembering where information lives rather than making interesting decisions.
