## 1. Mechanisms with sources

### 1.1 Lengnick 2013, JEBO 86, 102-120 (OPENED)
URL: https://legacy.econ.tuwien.ac.at/lva/compeco.se/artikel/jebo_2013_agent_based_macroeconomics_a_baseline_model.pdf

- Price formation: posted prices, firm-specific, adjusted only when inventory is off target. "A change of prices is only considered if the firm is confronted with an unsatisfying level of inventories." Inventory bounds are multiples of last month's demand (eqs 6-7). The price may rise only while it stays under an upper bar and fall only while above a lower bar, "given relative to marginal costs" (eqs 8-9: bars are phi times marginal cost with 1 < phi_low < phi_high). Step is a random draw (eq 10, p_new = p_old(1 +/- theta), theta ~ U[0, upsilon]), applied with a Calvo-style probability below one.
- Wage: raised if a vacancy went unfilled last month, cut if all positions stayed filled for gamma months, by a random uniform step (eq 5).
- Hiring: "If the inventory has fallen below [the lower bound] a new open position is created... If... above [the upper bound], a randomly chosen worker is fired", firing with a one month lag.
- Matching (decentralised search): each household knows only the prices of about 7 firms it is connected to; each month, with some probability, it compares one connected firm with one random unconnected firm (picked in proportion to firm size) and switches if the new price is at least a set percentage lower. A household that was rationed drops the firm that could not serve it. Unemployed households visit random firms until beta firms are seen, and accept a vacancy paying more than their reservation wage. Reservation wage falls 10 percent after a month unemployed.
- Stationary behaviour: simulation of 1000 households, 100 firms, "500 years (6000 months) plus a burn-in of 1000 months to get rid of the influence of arbitrary starting conditions". Result: unsatisfied demand under 0.03 percent in 95 percent of periods, unemployment between 0 and 4.3 percent, with an endogenous cycle. Robustness: "the equilibrium breaks down only if price flexibility becomes too low" and a larger set of connections (more price information) improves self-organisation of the goods market.
- Firm exit: none. Losses are absorbed by a buffer, then by an immediate wage cut; "This simplification allows to keep the number of firms constant and neglect firm entry and exit."
- Money: closed exchange economy, no credit and no interest (interest on savings normalised to 0). No bank.

### 1.2 Caiani, Godin, Caverzasi, Gallegati, Kinsella, Stiglitz 2016, JEDC 69, 375-408 (OPENED)
URL: https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/Joseph_Stiglitz/Agent%20based-stock%20flow.pdf

- Matching protocol on every market: "demand agents are allowed to observe the prices or the interest rates charged by a random subset of suppliers (whose size depends on a parameter chi...). Agents switch from the old partner to the best potential partner... with a probability Prs... a non-linear function of the percentage difference in their prices", governed by an "intensity of choice" parameter.
- Price (eq 3.6-3.7): price = (1 + markup) x expected unit labour cost. The markup is revised each period: lowered by a folded-normal draw when last period's inventories exceeded the desired share of expected sales, raised when below. Expectations follow a one-parameter adaptive rule z_e(t) = z_e(t-1) + lambda (z(t-1) - z_e(t-1)) (eq 3.1).
- Output: desired output = expected sales x (1 + nu) minus inventories (eq 3.2); labour demand follows, with excess workers randomly fired.
- Bank interest (eq 3.12): the loan rate is last period's rate times (1 + FN) if the bank's capital ratio is below the sector target, times (1 - FN) otherwise, FN a folded normal draw, anchored on the market average. Deposit rate moves the same way on the liquidity ratio (eq 3.14). Credit is rationed case by case from a default probability computed from operating cash flow against debt service (eq 3.13), not only priced. A central bank gives cash advances at a constant rate that "represents the upper bound for interest paid by banks on customers' deposits".
- Bankruptcy and entry: firms and banks fail on illiquidity or negative net worth. "For simplicity... defaulted firms and banks [are] bailed in by households... in order to maintain the number of firms and banks constant." Loss goes first to the firm's owner via fire sales of capital, the remainder to the bank; a failed bank is recapitalised by cutting deposits until its capital ratio reaches the minimum.
- Stock-flow consistency and initial conditions: a six step procedure. (1) derive an aggregate version; (2) constrain it to "a real stationary state associated with a nominal steady growth equal to g_ss"; (3) solve it numerically with exogenous empirical values (unemployment, markups, interest rates); (4) spread sector totals uniformly over agents; (5) back out original amounts and ages of loans and capital by assuming constant real value each past period; (6) assign matching partners so each supplier has equal customers. They state that models need "sufficiently high buffer stocks (e.g. capital, deposits, reserves) to face possible strange or extreme dynamics originated in this phase, possibly inducing the economy to snowball."
- Dynamics after that start: "does not imply in any way that the model dynamics sticks to the steady state employed in the calibration". The model passes through expansion and recession trends, then "converging, in most cases, to a relatively stable configuration... a 'stochastic steady-state'... or 'quasi steady-state'". They warn against simply discarding the transient.

### 1.3 Wiese et al. 2024 (arXiv 2409.18760), a calibrated descendant of Poledna et al. (OPENED)
URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18760

- Firm price (eq 8): P_f(t) = (1 + predicted PPI inflation) x (1 + demand-pull term) x (1 + cost-push term) x P_f(t-1). Predicted demand is last observed demand times a growth forecast (eq 7). Expectations are a lag-one autoregression.
- "Banks set the interest rates they offer... as a markup on the current Central Bank rate. This markup may also depend on the borrower's financial health." Footnote: Poledna et al. 2023 and Hommes et al. 2022 "assume fixed markups on the policy rate. In Assenza et al. (2015), the interest rate increases with the borrower's leverage; in Seppecher (2012), the interest rate is fixed."
- Search and matching on goods, labour, credit and housing; firms "under stress may file for bankruptcy", banks go bankrupt when equity is non positive and depositors are bailed in. Initial balance sheets are set from national accounts data and banks are matched to firms at initialisation by a linear sum assignment on initial states. "We set all initial prices to 1".
- Replacement on exit (arXiv 2501.19168, search result only, SNIPPET): one for one replacement, new firm a copy of an incumbent with no debt.

### 1.4 Peters et al. 2022, "Mak(h)ro 0" (arXiv 2205.00752) (OPENED)
URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00752

- Firm rule (eq 1): if output is at or below demand and the price is at or above the average price, raise output; if below the average price, raise price; if output exceeds demand and the price is above average, cut price; if the price is at or below average, cut output. "The cost-covering price for each firm is based on wage and borrowing cost per unit and defines the minimum price." Prices and quantities move by a gamma times a U[0,1] draw.
- Households pick a firm with a weight of average price over the firm's price, "to model incomplete price information", so households "usually will not be able to find the one with the lowest price". Loan rate to firms is the key rate plus a fixed standing facility spread plus an individual bank markup. Firm bankruptcy is a debt ratio rule; bankrupt firms are not reborn.

### 1.5 Lavoie 2007, Post-Keynesian SFC model (OPENED)
URL: https://www.depfe.unam.mx/actividades/11/curso-seccareccia/Lavoie2007.pdf

- "Our firms follow normal-cost pricing procedures, based on an estimate of normal historical unit costs... Pricing also adjusts partially." The mark-up depends on planned retained earnings.
- Bank rates: "with the liquidity ratio beyond the upper threshold, banks reduce the deposit rate in an attempt to get the liquidity ratio back to its normal range, and the lending rate follows". On a default shock, "the spread between the lending rate and the deposit rate is pushed up; in other words, banks raise their profit margins" to restore the capital adequacy ratio.
- Closure choice: "real government expenditures grow at a rate which is compatible over the long period with a constant rate of unemployment". Steady state values are computed and shifts between steady states are shown.
- Godley and Lavoie's book itself: NONE (not opened). SNIPPET only: choosing stock-flow norms "is a black art" (a search result quoting a Levy Institute paper).

### 1.6 Petrongolo and Pissarides 2001, JEL (OPENED)
URL: https://personal.lse.ac.uk/petrongo/jel-final.pdf

- Aggregate matching function M = m(U, V), increasing, concave, usually homogeneous of degree one; in discrete time M <= min(U, V). A worker finds a job with probability m(U,V)/U, a vacancy fills with probability m(U,V)/V. "In the absence of frictions, M = min(U,V)". Elasticities measure congestion (eta_U - 1) and thick market effects (eta_V).
- This is the closed form behind the "MATCHING_EFFICIENCY" and "MATCHING_SEARCHER_ELASTICITY" constants in `sim/labour/market/clearing.py`.

### (b) Extreme prices in thin markets, and prices that persist where nothing trades
1. `sim/economy/seller_offers.py:206-211` (`substitution_markup`): the cap on a seller's ask is cost x elasticity/(elasticity - 1), and is `math.inf` when elasticity <= 1. Used at line 246 as `limit`. Lengnick's analogue is a finite pair of bars phi x marginal cost on both sides, for every firm, always (Lengnick eq 8-9). Here a seller who "moves the price" in a thin market, facing buyers with a floor quantity (`goods_market.py:45-54`, floor plus flexible part), has residual demand that is steeply inelastic, so the profit-maximising ask runs to its ceiling every year (`seller_pricing.py:111-128`). The raise per year is bounded to 5 percent (`ask_raise.py:10`), but compounds for decades, and the comments at `seller_offers.py:167-170` say the unbounded profit is exactly why the hold exists.
2. `sim/economy/seller_pricing.py:21-31` and `seller_offers.py:222-253`: a dominant-firm optimiser searching 48 prices against the full book of buyers' schedules. Models above do not give a seller the buyers' demand curve: they give a markup rule on cost plus an inventory or sales signal (Caiani eq 3.6-3.7, Lengnick eq 8-10, Wiese eq 8). The optimiser needs perfect information about every bid, which also makes each seller's ask a function of every other seller's ask and so adds a feedback loop between years (point (a)).
3. `sim/economy/goods_market.py:12-14, 183-200`: when no offers exist or no demand exists the market returns `held_price` (the last price), or the lowest reservation. With no trade, `market_memory.py:59-64` forgets `cleared_prices` but `prices` (line 46) keeps the old value, and `notional.py` is the only guard for display. In none of the opened models does a price persist with no trade as a transaction price: Lengnick and Caiani set price from cost and inventory each period, so a firm with no sales keeps a cost based price, never a stale one; Peters et al. bound it below by the cost-covering price.
4. `sim/economy/goods_market.py:187` effective reservation = max(reservation, minimum_price), where minimum_price is 0.001 of the mean bid reference price (`goods_market.py:31-37, 85`); waste goods therefore clear at a token price that can be tens of times under cost, then shown. The opened ABMs have no zero cost sales except through the bankruptcy buffer.
5. Unsold search `sim/economy/unsold_search.py:91-101` floors the ask at `unit_value`, the lower of full cost and reservation (`seller_offers.py:201-203`): the floor is not the cost covering price of Peters et al. (their minimum is the full unit cost including borrowing). Under a price taker the ask may fall under cost forever.

### (a) Never settles
1. Annual step with one clearing per year: `docs/architecture/ECONOMY_AGENTS.md` "Annual step". Lengnick uses 21 daily visits within a month (Lengnick 2.3) so rationed buyers re-search (switch firm) inside the period; here the only within year feedback is the single bisection of `unsold_search.py`. Years of delay between a signal and the price response produce a cobweb: the doc itself says output is "decided on last year's price (a cobweb lag)".
2. Several overlapping speeds, none derived: ask raise 0.05 (`ask_raise.py:10`), worker ask 0.15 (`labour/market/asks.py:47`), wage closure 0.3 (`labour/market/clearing.py:13`), interest closure 0.3 (`credit.py:20`), usual price 0.1 (`market_memory.py:30`), volume 0.3 (`market_memory.py:23`). Caiani note the stable zone only holds for jointly calibrated parameters ("parameters... derived in order to be compatible with the SFC initial setup. Changing their values would impose an exogenous shock"). Here every one is flagged "temporary_heuristic, confidence D".
3. Asymmetric price rules: the seller optimiser raises (up to 5 percent a year) while unsold search cuts all searchable asks to a common level by bisection (`unsold_search.py:7-10`). A ratchet up plus a jump down is a limit cycle by construction; Lengnick's random symmetric step and bounded markup band are not.
4. Initial state: a hidden spin-up (`sim/economy/api.py:344-377`, doc "Start") runs until "prices and the interest rate settle". Caiani instead solve a steady state analytically and start every agent on it. Here, if the dynamics have no fixed point (item 1 and 3 above), the spin-up cannot settle however long it runs. Caiani also show a transient that is itself nontrivial even from a steady-state start.

### (c) Interest rate to zero
1. `sim/economy/lending.py:64`: working-capital funds are offered at `max(0.0, expected_inflation)`, with no time-preference term. The household offer in `households_orders.py:279-280` is `max(0, 0.05 + expected_inflation)`. When the working-capital pool is the marginal lender, or inflation expectations are negative, the offer minimum is 0.
2. `sim/economy/credit.py:93-113`: rate = sticky move toward a clearing point of lenders' floors and borrowers' caps. With savings plentiful, supply at the lowest ask exceeds demand, so the clearing point is the lowest ask (`clearing_point` docstring: "a glut of sellers holds the price at their floor"), and line 113 clips at zero. There is no rule that a lender must cover its cost or loss rate.
3. `sim/economy/banks.py:141-147`: bank ask = mean deposit rate + margin, with margin a temporary heuristic retiring on Complaint 106; deposit rate is the households' ask. No bank capital target, no funding cost beyond deposits, no expected loss; so no force raises the spread when defaults rise. Caiani eq 3.12 moves the rate on the bank's capital ratio; Lavoie raises the spread after defaults; Wiese uses a markup on a policy rate; Peters uses key rate + fixed spread + markup. All four anchor the loan rate to something that is not the clearing of a glut. The simulator has no central bank or policy rate and the clearing is the only anchor.
4. `sim/economy/credit.py:35-40, 215-218` and `banks.py:run_on_insolvent`: banks fail, but a lender's default loss does not feed a risk premium that is bank specific; `risk_premium` (credit.py:52-58) is borrower specific only.

### (d) 95 percent of offered labour hours unhired
1. Supply side: the whole population's hours are offered each year (`labour_inputs.py`, doc "Workers ask..."), while demand is a handful of producer bids derived from expected sales (`labour_bids.py`, tranches). In Lengnick each household offers one unit of labour and a firm's headcount follows its inventory signal one worker a month (Lengnick 2.2, footnote 18: up to 10 percent of the population per month), so firms add jobs until inventories stop falling. Here, a producer hires to the run plan, and a peasant household's own plot takes most hours, so hours offered but not wanted are partly just subsistence time, which the model counts as "unhired" rather than as out of the labour market.
2. The worker ask falls by at most 0.15 x unsold share a year and never under the own-plot floor (`labour/market/asks.py:62-86`, `labour_ask_floor.py`), so a market with a floor above the marginal product of the last hours stays at that floor and the hours stay unhired forever. There is no vacancy creating response to a low wage: Lengnick creates a vacancy when inventories are low, and a lower wage only matters through the cost bar on price. Here demand for hours is price driven only via the producers' maximum wage bid.
3. `sim/labour/market/clearing.py:84-98` (`market_wage`): with no bids the wage "drifts down to the ask", i.e. to the reservation wage, and with bids at `clearing_target` it is the midpoint of the marginal buyer and seller. Neither creates new employers. Entry (`entry.py`) is gated on goods market margins, not on a standing pool of idle hands, and `docs/architecture/ECONOMY_AGENTS.md` states idle hands are let go "instead of flooding back on a price spike".
4. The matching constants (`clearing.py:19-45`) follow Petrongolo and Pissarides' functional form only in outline: their M <= min(U, V) bound and congestion elasticity are in the paper, but the constant 2.0 per year is flagged "not fitted".

## 3. Ranked design changes

Ranking is by expected effect on the four symptoms against implementation cost. Stability notes only repeat what the opened sources say.

1. Replace the dominant-firm optimiser as the default ask rule by a cost-anchored band plus an inventory/sales signal (Lengnick eq 6-10; Caiani eq 3.6-3.7; Peters eq 1). Ask is bounded below by full unit cost (as in Peters) and above by a finite multiple; raise it when sold out, cut it when stock is left, in symmetric random steps with a Calvo probability. Keep the optimiser only for a genuine monopoly with an explicit price cap. Source behaviour: Lengnick find near-equilibrium (unsatisfied demand under 0.03 percent in 95 percent of periods) and an endogenous cycle; Lengnick state the equilibrium "breaks down only if price flexibility becomes too low", so the step must not be set too small. Fixes (b) and much of (a). Touches `seller_offers.py`, `seller_pricing.py`, `ask_raise.py`.
2. Make a shown price a transaction price or a cost anchored quote, never a remembered one: when a market does not clear, set the quote to the cheapest seller's cost covering ask (Peters), or the markup price (Caiani), and let `prices` and `usual_prices` be updated from that. Source: Caiani and Peters both rebuild prices from costs each period, so an untraded market has no stale price. Fixes the second half of (b). Touches `goods_market.py:183-200`, `market_memory.py`, `notional.py`.
3. Add a within year re-search among partners: within the year split clearing into several rounds (Lengnick: 21 days; Caiani: each period a random subset of suppliers is observed and partners switch with a probability rising in the price gap). The sources report better self-organisation with more price information (Lengnick Fig 14: five or more connections). Cost: more clears per market per year; the existing `Buyers` caching could amortise it. Reduces cobweb lag, helps (a) and (d).
4. Anchor the interest rate and give lenders a floor above zero: (i) lender floor = time preference + expected loss + operating margin (add the missing term in `lending.py:64`); (ii) bank loan rate moves with its capital ratio and spread rises after defaults (Caiani eq 3.12; Lavoie's capital adequacy adjustment); (iii) optionally an issuer or mint policy rate with a fixed markup (Wiese; Peters). Source stability: Lavoie reports the banks restore the capital ratio "within a short time period" by raising spreads; Caiani reports convergence to a quasi steady state. Fixes (c). Touches `credit.py`, `lending.py`, `banks.py`.
5. Initialise on a solved stationary state, not by spin-up alone (Caiani steps 1-6): derive the aggregate stationary state with constant real quantities and a chosen nominal growth, then distribute it, including loan ages from a constant real vintage assumption; endow agents with large buffers for the transient. Source: Caiani still observe a transient, and say not to discard it; but reported initial symmetric starts reach a quasi steady state in most runs. Gives a measurable pass/fail test for (a): after N years the price changes below a bound. Also a burn-in window is standard practice (Lengnick 1000 months; Caiani).
6. Tie labour demand to inventory/vacancy signals and let the wage follow the vacancy history (Lengnick eq 5-7: raise the wage if a vacancy went unfilled, cut it if all were filled; open a vacancy when stock is short). Use the Petrongolo-Pissarides form for hires, M = min(U, V, m(U,V)), with the congestion elasticity. Report unhired hours as the unemployment of the labour force only, separating subsistence plot hours. Fixes the measure and some of the dynamics in (d). Touches `labour_bids.py`, `labour/market/clearing.py`, `asks.py`.
7. Firm entry and exit: keep exit as is (cash repays lenders first, matches Caiani 3.3), and make entry replace exits one for one with a copy of an incumbent at average price and wage with no debt (Caiani and arXiv 2501.19168 snippet) when the market has no maker, plus the margin based entry already present. Source stability: both Lengnick and Caiani hold the firm count constant to avoid entry feedback; the simulator's own report (`entry_margin.py` docs) says the first attempt at margin entry "destabilised staple prices", consistent with that. Lower priority; do after 1-5.
8. Stock-flow consistency: the repo already keeps every movement of money with a named counterparty (doc "Stock-flow consistency"). Remaining gap is `edge:legacy` postings (Complaint 382). Caiani's transaction-flow matrix and "quadruple entry" checks (their Tables 1-2) are the model for an automatic yearly check, which would give an invariant test for item 5. No change to design needed.
