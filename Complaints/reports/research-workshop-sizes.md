## What the sourced figures say

1. **Same recipe, different scale by town.** Pompeii bakeries held 3 to 4 stones, Ostia's about 9 on far more floor,
   and the population served per stone rose from about 90 to 150-300. Workshop size and number are outcomes of the
   market, not recipe constants. This is the main argument against a typed per-recipe workplace size, which is what
   the reverted branch built.
2. **Local, bulky trades stay a crowd.** One Domesday mill per 46 households; tens of bakeries and a double-digit
   count of fulleries in one town; over 600 pottery workshops at La Graufesenque.
3. **Large units appear where plant or resource is the constraint.** Han foundries beside ore (28,000 square metres
   against under 10,000), Laurion contractors with hundreds of men, blast furnace against bloomery.
4. **Mixed structure is normal.** Large and small coexist: Han household, state and plantation units; Ostia's 3 large
   and 2 small fulleries; La Graufesenque's few dozen prominent among 600. One typical size per recipe misdescribes this.
5. **Modern firm sizes are heavy tailed.** A paper opened here, which cites Axtell, reports a power law with exponent
   about 1.06 on roughly 5.5 million US firms (1997), the Zipf case at exponent one
   ([P] https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0401053). Axtell's own Science paper was not reachable. No source ties this
   to antiquity; it is a modern shape, not a calibration target for Rome.

## What sets minimum efficient scale

All [theory, not opened] except where a table source is named.

- **Transport cost of output sets the catchment.** Dear-to-ship output (grain, flour) gives small reach and many
  shops (Domesday mills). Cheap-to-ship or high-value output (samian, exported oil amphorae) lets one site serve a
  continent, so fewer, bigger plants per unit demand.
- **Capital lumpiness sets the floor.** A kiln, furnace or millstone set is bought whole and wants running near
  capacity. Kiln capacity of tens of thousands of items per firing and the 3-4 vs 9 stone layouts show the lumps.
- **Owner supervision limits the ceiling.** Laurion shows ownership and management split (Nicias leasing 1,000 men to
  one contractor). No opened source quantifies span of control for other trades.
- **Guild and state rules.** The Han monopoly (117 BCE) put iron under offices (Lam et al., opened). No opened source
  gives a rule fixing workshop size or count, so do not model one.
- **Demand density.** Ostia against Pompeii: denser markets supported bigger workshops.

## How to model it

Goal: the number of competing sellers in a market follows from scale economies and market size, so strategic
(monopolist) pricing applies only where a market truly supports one seller.

1. **A physical minimum plant per recipe.** The smallest unit that can run (one kiln, one millstone set, one hearth,
   one press): output per period and fixed capital. These are technology facts in recipe data, like yields (4.5), not
   counts per tile. This is the "plant-scale datum" 550 lacks. It is a lower bound, not a typical size, because the
   same recipe appears at 3 and at 9 stones.
2. **Derive the count.** Demand reachable at delivered cost (route freight already lives in `sim/geography`) divided
   by minimum plant output bounds how many workshops the market can keep running; free entry stops where expected
   profit at that scale reaches zero [theory, not opened]. Grain: small reach, many shops. Samian, oil, iron near
   ore: large reach, few big plants.
3. **Let size rise with density.** Pick the plant multiple that minimises unit cost given a supervision cost for each
   added unit, so Pompeii and Ostia both fall out. The supervision term has no sourced value; it needs a labelled
   heuristic with a `retire` (4.4).
4. **Pricing follows count.** `stands_for_many` becomes true when derived N exceeds one, replacing the typed
   `Producer.workshops` counter that starts at one in the opening. A lone mine or a founder's plant (N of one) stays
   strategic.
5. **Tests.** The one 550 already asks for (split one aggregate into N identical producers: price and quantity
   unchanged within tolerance), plus: lowering a good's freight cost widens reach, lowers derived N, and widens
   markup in a thin market.

## Recommendation for 550

1. Do not reintroduce a typed per-recipe workplace size. Pompeii against Ostia, and 600 La Graufesenque workshops
   against a few dozen prominent, show a typed size is wrong for one recipe across town sizes.
2. Add a physical minimum plant per recipe and compute each opening producer's workshop count from tile demand within
   delivered-cost reach over plant output, replacing the opening's start at one workshop.
3. Keep `stands_for_many` as the switch, fed by derived N.
4. Treat the reverted merge's symptoms (founder's market rate up, thin ore market at scarcity price, iron opening at
   its floor) as separate bugs to trace. The evidence suggests few or one operator at ore-bound iron is plausible.
5. Get the crew data first: Monteix (Pompeii bakery staffing, in Flohr and Wilson, *Urban Craftsmen and Traders*,
   2016), Flohr (*The World of the Fullo*, 2013), Cleere and Crossley (Weald), Domergue (Vipasca, Rio Tinto),
   Marichal (La Graufesenque), Wagner (Han iron). Until then leave crew size unset; any labour-based scale is a
   labelled heuristic.
