# Reach and built roads are not read by the engine

**Status:** closed - roads, railways, canals, bridges and ports are built over time by a crew hired through the labour market, steep ground is built engineered, a train and a canal barge have carriage rates, market areas follow the built ways, the reach ladder is derived from the carriers' own days per tile, settlement shares people by the tiles' food potential, and a mine names a deposit its seat has found and is bounded by it (the one folded-in item left, surveyed positions per mine from 281, is deferred by the owner)

Geography routes over tiles by any mode a civilisation holds, with roads and railways as built improvements the caller records (`api.route`, `api.reach`, `api.edge_key`, `api.build_requirements`; `sim/geography/INTERFACE.md`).

## Done

- `state.economy.improvements` ({edge key: {way: true}}) is saved with the game. `Sim.build_way` / `way_quote` (`sim/engine/ways.py`, command `build_way`) price a road or railway from `api.build_requirements`: earthwork on the edge's grade (a bed plus a balanced cut-and-fill bench), surface and fixed materials from the mode's `construction` data (labelled heuristics), labour at the labour market, material at the goods market. Steeper ground costs more (ground beyond the natural limit: see the engineered ways below). `sim/tests/test_geography_ways_build.py`, `sim/tests/test_built_ways.py`.
- The agent economy's carriage reads the built ways: `EconomySetup.improvements` (handed in from `state.economy.improvements` at the opening, so the spin-up key covers them), `CarriageTable` passes them to geography's `route_costs`, a `road` carrier is priced in `_carrier_models`, and `Economy.set_improvements` (through `sim/economy/api.py`) rebuilds the carriage table only when the ways change; `AgentEconomy.sync_ways` runs at the start of each economy year. `sim/tests/test_economy_built_ways.py`, `sim/tests/test_port_built_ways.py`.
- Labour reach (`reachable_tiles`) and relocation pass the improvements. Relocation takes the days of `api.route(..., fastest=True)` over the modes held, not a straight-line pace; `RELOCATION_KM_PER_DAY` and the `base_reach` speed-up are gone, and a tile no route joins is refused. `sim/tests/test_relocation_follows_fastest_route.py`.
- `Geography.region_reach` is the fewest days of the fastest route from the tiles held to any tile of the region, over the modes and ways held, banded on a ladder derived from the carriers (see the last bullet of this list); a region no route joins is the farthest level. `material_reach` is calibrated against the same table for the civilisation the located-material costs were written for (`located_material_reference_civilisation`), replacing `reach_from_italia`. Reach and mineral access are rebuilt when the techniques held or the ways built change. `civilisation.base_reach` is deleted. `sim/tests/test_reach_from_tiles.py`.
- `material_freight_distance_km` is the km of geography's cheapest route to the nearest producing region (the great-circle distance between the nearest pair of tiles only when no mode joins them).
- The region id "italia" fallback in `economy_mining.py` is gone (a civilisation holding no tile has no woodland ceiling); `sim/tests/test_reach_from_tiles.py` fails if an engine or geography module names a region id.

- Bridges, ports, canals and engineered ways (`sim/geography/ways_build.py`, `ways_works.py`, `data/world/geography/ways/`, the `canal` mode in `route_modes/modes.json`): a land edge between tiles on the same river is a river crossing, which land modes ford at their own handling and a railway cannot cross without a bridge; a bridge is priced from the masonry and timber it places and recorded on the edge. A port is priced from its mole and quay, recorded under a coastal tile that has no natural harbour (`is_port`), and joins that tile to the sea edges of the bordering harbours. A canal is a built water way mode on a land edge (a barge on still water at the levelled grade). Ground steeper than a way's natural limit is built `engineered` (cuttings and embankments, the bench multiplied by the mode's earthwork factor) up to the mode's engineered limit, and the edge is recorded engineered so routes accept it. `build_way` and `way_quote` take all of these; `build_way port <tile>` names a port by one tile. The construction figures are labelled heuristics in the data. `sim/tests/test_geography_ways_works.py`, `sim/tests/test_ways_crew_and_works.py`.
- A train and a canal barge have carriage rates in the engine's carrier models (`_carrier_models` in `foreign_routes.py`): the train's physical inputs come from geography (`api.train_carrier`, `rail_freight.py`: rolling resistance energy at the engine's efficiency, crew hours, the stock's wear), priced with coal for fuel, the crew's wage and the rolling stock as capital; the canal barge is the river barge on still water. The agent economy can haul over a built railway or canal. `sim/tests/test_rail_and_canal_carriage_rates.py`.
- Market areas follow the ways: when the ways change the economy partitions its areas again over the new carriage table (`EconomySetup.area_map`) and moves what it remembers about each market to the area that now holds the old anchor (`sim/economy/market_areas_follow.py`); the record keeps the ways its areas were partitioned for, so a saved game resumes with the same areas. `sim/tests/test_market_areas_follow_ways.py`.
- A way's crew is hired through the labour market: the crew is the people the market finds of the way's trade (at most the work's crew), the build years follow from what that crew works in a year, the crew leans on the trade's pool every year of the build and is given back when the way opens (`sim/engine/ways.py`). The labelled heuristic that the labourers were not missed is gone.
- The reach ladder is derived from the carriers: the first band is the days the slowest coastal carrier takes over a typical coastal edge, and the ratio is the fastest carrier's pace over the slowest's spread over the steps the levels leave (`reach_bands.ladder`); `reach_band_first_days` and `reach_band_ratio` are deleted. `sim/tests/test_reach_ladder_from_carriers.py`.

Not run here: the whole-game checks (`sim/tests/test_built_ways.py`, `test_port_built_ways.py`, `test_reach_from_tiles.py`) need a game built, which takes too long in this container; the reach levels of each civilisation move with the new ladder and are to be re-measured with `python3 sim/simulator.py` on a whole game.
- Settlement reads food potential (414): a tile's share of its nation's people is its share of the nation's food potential, the sustainable food energy its crops, herds, game, wild plants and fish give (`api.food_potential`, the land at a full stock), so a coast or steppe tile with no arable land holds people. The gathering hours' cap and the game thinned by hunting (`society_food_potential`, Complaint 411) bound what a society takes in a year, not where its people live, so settlement does not read them. `sim/geography/settlement.py`, `sim/tests/test_settlement_reads_food_potential.py`.
- A mine names a found deposit and is bounded by it (415, 440): `state.holdings.deposits_found` keeps what prospecting found (`prospect` command, `api.prospect` seeded from the game's own dice) and `deposit_drawn` what each deposit has yielded. A deposit's size is the catalogue's endowment (the older deposit model's output over a working life where the catalogue gives none, labelled `DEPOSIT_ASSUMED_WORKING_LIFE_YEARS`); a deposit already worked before the game began has yielded its share (`mined_before`); a working may raise at most the working share of the size a year. `open_mine` allocates new capacity to named deposits (`deposit=`) or those with most room, each tranche and working carries the deposit id, each year's yield is drawn from it, the yield is capped by what is left, and a worked-out deposit closes its workings. The automatic mine prospects when no found deposit has room (`AUTO_PROSPECT_PERSON_DAYS`). `sim/engine/mine_deposits.py`, `sim/geography/resources_sites.py`, `sim/tests/test_mine_names_a_deposit.py`.
- The port hands the agent economy its sites (440): each ore recipe is sited on the tiles that hold a found deposit of its resource, at the runs a year the deposits' room allows (a worked-out tile keeps a limit of nothing), at the opening and each year; the ore the firms raised is read back from `YearOutcome.extraction` and drawn from the tile's deposits, the ledger the seat's mines draw on. Recipes for materials the catalogue lists no ore for (coal, salt, quarries) stay on the economy's labelled fallback. `sim/engine/economy_port_sites.py`, `sim/tests/test_firms_work_found_deposits.py`; whole-game check `sim/tests/test_mines_deposits_whole_game.py` (not run here).
- Not done, and why it is not asked: 281 (surveyed positions per mine) is marked owner: later. The region mineral tables and `mineral_scale` still feed the mine ceiling (an organisational limit that now sits beside the deposit bound), so the last `sim.world` import in geography stays; removing them is a follow-on, not a requirement of naming a deposit.
