# Node revenue was authored against book material costs, and solved costs are lower

**Status:** partly

With `data/prices.json` deleted, every node's material cost comes from the price solver. Authored `rev` figures (book denarii, converted through `money_units`) were written against the old book prices, which for many materials sit well above what the recipes imply. `python3 -c "import sys; sys.path.insert(0,'.'); from sim.engine import data; ..."` over `data.load()` shows the payback (`_total_cost / rev`) of the cheapest node fell: before, no node paid back its whole cost in under about three months; now dozens do, and the fastest two (`el2_three_wire_distribution_system`, `el2_ring_main_distribution`) pay back in about two weeks. `sim/tests/test_early_playtest.py` had to lower `PUMP_PAYBACK_YEARS_FLOOR` to let the check pass.

Why it matters: revenue-to-cost payback drives which projects the player prefers, so a node whose cost fell while its authored revenue stayed is a free-money lever (CLAUDE.md 4.1: revenue should fall out of what the node produces, not an authored number).

Resolution on this branch: `sim/engine/node_revenue.py` sets each node's `rev_hours` at load. A node declaring `annual_output_t` and gating production entries earns that output (split evenly over the materials it makes) at solved prices. Every other node keeps its authored `rev_hours`, held to at most its whole cost over `MINIMUM_PAYBACK_YEARS` (a labelled temporary heuristic). `PUMP_PAYBACK_YEARS_FLOOR` in `test_early_playtest.py` is back at its original value.

Update (node-revenue-from-output): `sim/engine/node_output.py` derives revenue for every node that gates production entries and whose staff or plant gives a physical output: output a year is the smaller of the plant's `annual_output_at_basis` and the staff's hours (`sch` + `art` people) over the entry's hours a unit, split evenly in hours across product lines, held to `annual_output_t` where declared; revenue is that output at solved prices less the materials it buys. `Sim.node_output_market_factor` scales it each year by the market's price over long-run cost for what it sells and buys. Sciences that make nothing earn nothing. Each node's `_revenue_basis` says which of `output`, `knowledge` or `authored` applies (counts: `python3 -c` over `data.load()`).

What remains: the nodes still `authored` (equipment, institutions, services, and the gated nodes with no staff or plant to derive from) keep their authored figure under the payback cap, so it still binds on some and the `test_early_playtest` floor stays tautological for them. Energy bought and sold is not valued (Complaints/350); upkeep is still authored and disagrees with the derived revenue (Complaints/351). Product definitions for services (fees from the population served, share of trade enabled) and for what a machine's work is worth are the next step. Related: Complaints/123, Complaints/288.

**Also:** while `MINIMUM_PAYBACK_YEARS` caps revenue at the same quarter-year that `test_early_playtest.py`'s `PUMP_PAYBACK_YEARS_FLOOR` checks, that check cannot fail: it guards the cap, not the economy. When revenue comes from output for every node, delete the cap and the check becomes a real guard again. `python3 -c` over `node_revenue` listing nodes whose `rev` equals the cap measures how much still rests on it.

Update (revenue-and-upkeep): the nodes that make something now also derive their upkeep (`sim/engine/node_upkeep.py`: staff at the civilisation's wages for the hours their lines work, plus a labelled share of what the plant their entries state cost to build, never more than the wear the solved price already charges), energy is valued on both sides at the price graded to each entry (`sim/engine/energy_prices.py`, Complaints/350), and every figure is derived for the civilisation playing (`node_revenue.for_civilisation`, Complaints/421). `python3 sim/node_revenue_report.py [--civ ID] [--list]` prints the counts by basis (revenue and upkeep), the nodes still held under the payback floor, the payback distributions and the nodes earning less than their upkeep.

What stays authored, and why: no production entry names a product for the equipment, institution, measurement, vehicle and service nodes that are most of the `authored` class (a ferry's crossings, a toll bridge's traffic, a mill's grain, a school's fees), so there is nothing physical to derive revenue or upkeep from. A mill needs a milling entry (wheat in, flour out, the mechanical work, the stones' throughput) and a source of wind or tide power in the energy entries; a ferry or bridge needs the traffic a crossing serves, which depends on the population and trade around it. Gated nodes that do have entries but no plant capacity, staff hours or declared output (Complaints/461) cannot be bounded. The payback floor therefore still binds on the nodes `node_revenue_report.py --list` names for the civilisation, so `PUMP_PAYBACK_YEARS_FLOOR` in `test_early_playtest.py` stays tautological for them; delete `MINIMUM_PAYBACK_YEARS` when that list is empty. Upkeep of those nodes also stays authored and some earn less than it (`--list` names them); it follows their revenue once they have a product.

Update (concern-margins-and-capital-charge): two more makers derive their revenue from output (bleaching powder, hand papermaking); counts from `python3 sim/node_revenue_report.py`. `PUMP_PAYBACK_YEARS_FLOOR` stays at the same value as `MINIMUM_PAYBACK_YEARS`; the guard is not stricter than the heuristic floor it tests.
