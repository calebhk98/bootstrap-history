# The local labour market cannot shrink past a floor, so it outlives the country it sits in

## What the player saw

Playing Mexica from 1500 under repeated epidemic and conquest shocks, national
population fell through a series of catastrophic values: roughly 5,542, then
1,389, then 182, then roughly 30 people. At every one of those points the
`population` command kept reporting a home town of about 14,375 people and
labour pools such as "100-200+ reachable artisans" - at the end, a local
artisan pool vastly larger than the country's own estimated total of
artisans. The command's own text says the local and national columns are
different concepts that "do not disagree." Under this much collapse they
plainly do: the local abstraction is bigger than the surviving country.

## Verified against current code

The mechanism is exactly what the player's own hypothesis pointed at, one
level deeper. Everything under `population` is built in
`sim/engine/labour_population.py` (split out of the old `labour.py`, which is
where the player's notes still name it).

`home_town_population_estimate()`:

    return self.TOWN_POPULATION_REFERENCE * (self.POP_SCALE_FLOOR_SHARE
                                              + self.POP_SCALE_VARIABLE_SHARE * min(1.0, self.pop_scale))

`market_supply()` (the function `reachable_trade_population()` divides by
`HOURS_PER_PERSON_YEAR` to get the "within your reach" column) uses the
identical `town = TOWN_POPULATION_REFERENCE * (POP_SCALE_FLOOR_SHARE +
POP_SCALE_VARIABLE_SHARE * min(1.0, pop_scale))` expression for the
"abundant"/"common" trade classes.

`pop_scale` itself, in `sim/engine/core.py`:

    @property
    def pop_scale(self):
        return max(self.POP_SCALE_FLOOR, self.population.total / self.DEFAULT_POPULATION_100AD)

`POP_SCALE_FLOOR` is declared at 0.05 (`core.py:245`) - "so a tiny starting
civilisation population never divides a formula by something vanishingly
small." `POP_SCALE_FLOOR_SHARE` (0.25) and `POP_SCALE_VARIABLE_SHARE` (0.75)
are declared in `sim/engine/labour_capacity.py:175-193`.

Put together: no matter how far `self.population.total` (the real, live
age-cohort headcount) falls, `pop_scale` never drops below 0.05, so
`home_town_population_estimate()` never drops below:

    50000 * (0.25 + 0.75 * min(1.0, 0.05)) = 14375.0

Checked directly:

    python3 -c "print(50000*(0.25+0.75*min(1.0,0.05)))"
    14374.999999999998

That is the player's own number, to the decimal. It is not an approximation
of the bug, it is the bug's exact output, and it is the same value at
population 5,542 and at population 30, because `pop_scale` is floored before
either figure is used.

The "100-200+ reachable artisans" figure is the same arithmetic one level
down. `reachable_trade_population()` for a "common" trade class is
`town * TRADE_DENSITY_COMMON` and for "abundant" is `town * TRADE_DENSITY_ABUNDANT`
(0.007 and 0.014 respectively, `labour_population.py:341-350`):

    python3 -c "print(14375*0.007, 14375*0.014)"
    100.625 201.25

Also exact matches for the reported range.

Meanwhile `national_trade_population()` reads `self.population.total`
directly, with no floor:

    pop = self.population.total
    ...
    return urban * self.TRADE_DENSITY.get(self._trade_market_class(t), 0.0)

so at population 30, "estimated in the country" for a common trade correctly
collapses toward zero while "within your reach" stays pinned at ~100. That is
the exact contradiction the player is describing, and it is generated by two
different functions consulting two differently-behaved population numbers -
one real and unfloored, one floored and therefore fictitious once the real
number falls far enough below the floor's own effective size.

The functions themselves know this is a defensible-until-it-isn't modelling
choice: `POP_SCALE_FLOOR`'s own declaration calls itself "a guard value, not
a demographic claim." It was written to stop a division blowing up on a
tiny *starting* population, not to survive a wartime collapse to 30 people
happening mid-run. The player's finding is that the guard rail has become
the load-bearing wall.

Status: **confirmed in current code**, and reproduced to the exact displayed
figures without needing to run a scenario - the arithmetic is deterministic
and small enough to check directly against the constants as declared.

## Cross-references

**`Complaints/18-plague-population-display.md`** - the player names this
overlap themselves. `18` is about the *national* population figure lagging a
just-announced plague until the next refresh (a staleness/timing bug in

even with `population.total` fully live and correctly refreshed, the *local
market* estimates built from `pop_scale` still cannot fall below their floor.
Fixing `18` does nothing for this one, and this one was not previously filed.
Distinct defects, adjacent subsystem.

## What would resolve it

The player's own suggested invariant is right and belongs in
`sim/tests/`:

    home_town_population_estimate() <= population.total
    reachable_trade_population(t)   <= plausible national_trade_population(t) + founder-trained exceptions

This is compatible with CLAUDE.md SS3.1/SS3.2: nothing here is a historical
outcome being hardcoded, it is a floor value that was reasonable at
initialisation and stopped being reasonable at extreme values nobody checked
against. Two honest ways to fix it, in increasing order of change:

1. **Drop the floor at the low end, or make it track the collapse.** Replace
   `POP_SCALE_FLOOR` as a fixed 0.05 with a floor that is itself a small,
   fixed *fraction of the currently surviving population* rather than a
   fixed fraction of Rome's reference size - so "a quarter of Rome's own
   town depth" (the `POP_SCALE_FLOOR_SHARE` comment) degrades gracefully
   into "a quarter of what a town this size could plausibly hold" instead of
   pinning at a constant absolute headcount forever.
2. **Cap the local estimate at the national one directly**, the simplest
   fix and the one the player's invariant literally states:
   `home_town_population_estimate()` and `reachable_trade_population()`
   each take `min(their own formula, self.population.total)` (or the
   national trade estimate, for the per-trade figure) before returning.
   Cheaper to implement, and it cannot let the local number outlive the
   national one again by construction, regardless of what `pop_scale`'s
   floor is tuned to elsewhere (hiring caps, wage pressure, etc., which this
   complaint does not touch).

Either way, add a regression test that drives `self.population.total` to a
low value directly (no need to simulate an actual epidemic chain - the
existing `sim.tests.harness` pattern of constructing a `Sim` and poking
`s.population` cohorts, as `test_demographics_and_plague.py` already does,
is enough) and asserts the invariant above.

## The invariant

    home_town_population_estimate() <= population.total
    reachable_trade_population(t)   <= plausible national_trade_population(t)

Already named in the player's own ARCH-002 section, and worth pinning as a
`sim/tests/` check specifically because - as this file and `70` both show -
its violation is not confined to one screen.
